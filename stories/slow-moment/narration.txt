What if you could slow down any moment you wanted?
Just once a day. You press your thumb and finger together... and the world goes slow.
You try it in the lunch queue. Boring. Slow boring.
You try it on a rollercoaster. Amazing.
You try it when your little sister laughs so hard milk comes out of her nose. Perfect.
Here's the strange part. Scientists think the real world already does this to you.
In two thousand and seven, a neuroscientist called David Eagleman dropped volunteers backwards from a tower, thirty-one metres, into a net.
Afterwards, they said their own fall lasted about a third longer than anyone else's.
But while they fell, they couldn't read a fast flashing number any better than normal.
Time didn't slow down. Their memory just recorded more.
Scary or new moments get stored in more detail... so they feel longer when you look back.
That's why the first day of a holiday lasts forever, and an ordinary week vanishes.
So you stop saving your slow moment for rollercoasters.
You use it on Grandma's kitchen. On your dad's terrible singing. On the first snow.
And the year, somehow, starts to feel longer.
